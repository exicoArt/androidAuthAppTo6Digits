# Safe money checklist

General habits, not financial or legal advice. For your own situation, talk to your bank or a qualified advisor.

## Before you send or approve anything

- [ ] I started this contact myself (I did not reply to a message, call or link).
- [ ] I checked the recipient's details through a second channel I trust.
- [ ] Nobody is rushing me. Pressure and secrecy are red flags.
- [ ] I'm not being asked to pay with gift cards, crypto or a wire transfer to "fix" a problem.
- [ ] I'm not paying a fee to receive a prize, loan, job or refund.

## Accounts

- [ ] 2FA is on for my bank, email, payment apps and any exchange.
- [ ] My **email** account is the best protected of all, because it can reset the others.
- [ ] I use unique passwords from a password manager.
- [ ] Backup codes are stored offline.
- [ ] Transaction alerts are on, with limits that fit how I really spend.

## Common scams to recognise

- **Fake support:** someone says your account is compromised and asks for a code or remote access. Real banks don't ask for codes.
- **Investment or romance scams:** guaranteed returns, a new online friend who needs money, pressure to move to another app.
- **Package and toll messages:** a link to pay a small fee, leading to a fake payment page.
- **Impersonation of family or bosses:** a new number asking for urgent money. Call the known number.

## If something goes wrong

1. Contact your bank or payment provider right away, using the number on your card or official app.
2. Change passwords starting with email, and revoke the authenticator and sessions if you think the device was exposed.
3. Report the scam to the platform and to your local consumer or cybercrime authority.
4. Keep evidence (messages, URLs, transaction IDs).
